Selective decoding for streaming video

Video that speaks only when something happens.

LiveJEPA turns live video into a stream of meaning. While the meaning holds still, the language decoder stays off. When it moves, LiveJEPA writes one sentence.

How it works

Three models in a row. Only the middle one learns.

LiveJEPA rebuilds the selective decoding idea from VL-JEPA, whose code and weights were never released, out of public parts: a frozen video encoder, a frozen sentence decoder, and one small predictor trained to connect them.

The predictor is the only part we trained, on 26,360 COIN clips. Everything it outputs lands in a space the decoder already understands.

Meaning space

It thinks in sentences, not pixels.

The predictor places every window at a point in SONAR space. Nearby points mean nearly the same thing, so a steady scene keeps the point inside one region and the decoder has nothing new to say.

Move your pointer across the map. Inside a region LiveJEPA stays quiet. Cross into a new one and it speaks once.

What we found

Averaging is the real win.

We matched every decode budget exactly and compared three schedules on the paper's own benchmark, EgoExo4D atomic descriptions. Decoding the average of a span instead of a single window lifted caption quality at every budget. With the same averaging, evenly spaced decodes did as well as adaptive ones.

We report that as the finding. On clean, redundant streams adaptive placement still saves decoder calls at equal quality.
